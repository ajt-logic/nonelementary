Inline symbols

In first-order logic we write $\Gamma \vDash \varphi$ for semantic entailment and $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ for syntactic derivability. Soundness says $\Gamma \vdash \varphi$ implies $\Gamma \vDash \varphi$; completeness gives the converse. Quantifiers $\forall x$ and $\exists x$ range over a domain, $a \in D$ is domain membership, $A \subseteq B$ is substructure inclusion, and set-builder notation like $\{x : \varphi(x)\}$ picks out a definable subset.

A displayed equation

The compactness theorem:

$$\Gamma \vDash \varphi \iff \text{there is a finite } \Gamma_0 \subseteq \Gamma \text{ such that } \Gamma_0 \vDash \varphi$$

And soundness/completeness together, as an aligned pair:

$$\begin{aligned} \Gamma \vdash \varphi &\implies \Gamma \vDash \varphi \quad \text{(soundness)} \\ \Gamma \vDash \varphi &\implies \Gamma \vdash \varphi \quad \text{(completeness)} \end{aligned}$$

Substitution, subscripts and superscripts

The substitution axiom schema of first-order logic:

$$\forall x\, \alpha \rightarrow \alpha^{x}_{t}$$

Here $\alpha^{x}_{t}$ denotes the formula obtained by replacing every free occurrence of $x$ in $\alpha$ with the term $t$, provided $t$ is substitutable for $x$ in $\alpha$ (no free variable of $t$ becomes bound). Substitutions can stack: $\big(\alpha^{x}_{t}\big)^{y}_{s} = \alpha^{x,y}_{t,s}$ when $x \neq y$ and neither substitution captures the other's variables.

A short numbered proof

Claim: $\vdash \alpha \rightarrow \alpha$, from the Hilbert-style axioms

$$A_1:\ \alpha \rightarrow (\beta \rightarrow \alpha), \qquad A_2:\ \big(\alpha \rightarrow (\beta \rightarrow \gamma)\big) \rightarrow \big((\alpha \rightarrow \beta) \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \gamma)\big)$$

and modus ponens (MP).

  1. $\alpha \rightarrow \big((\alpha \rightarrow \alpha) \rightarrow \alpha\big)$ — instance of $A_1$, with $\beta := \alpha \rightarrow \alpha$.
  2. $\big(\alpha \rightarrow ((\alpha \rightarrow \alpha) \rightarrow \alpha)\big) \rightarrow \big((\alpha \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \alpha)) \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \alpha)\big)$ — instance of $A_2$, with $\beta := \alpha \rightarrow \alpha$, $\gamma := \alpha$.
  3. $\big(\alpha \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \alpha)\big) \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \alpha)$ — MP on 1, 2.
  4. $\alpha \rightarrow (\alpha \rightarrow \alpha)$ — instance of $A_1$, with $\beta := \alpha$.
  5. $\alpha \rightarrow \alpha$ — MP on 4, 3.

$\blacksquare$
